## Environmental Science — Isotopes as Environmental Clocks

Ratios of naturally occurring isotopes act as a record of environmental conditions:

- The ratio of oxygen-18 to oxygen-16 trapped in ancient ice cores changes with past ocean temperatures, letting scientists reconstruct historical climate records.
- Carbon isotope ratios in tree rings and sediment layers reveal past atmospheric CO₂ conditions and plant growth patterns.

None of this would be possible without the core idea from this chapter: isotopes of the same element are chemically identical, so they get incorporated into ice, shells, and wood in the same way — but their differing masses leave a physically measurable signature behind.
