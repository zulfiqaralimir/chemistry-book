## Physics — Mass Spectrometry and Nuclear Physics

A **mass spectrometer** separates isotopes by firing ionised atoms through a magnetic field. Because heavier ions curve less sharply than lighter ones for the same speed and charge, the instrument sorts atoms purely by mass-to-charge ratio — a direct physical measurement of the isotope masses introduced in Chapter 3.

Atomic structure is also the foundation of **nuclear physics**: radioactive decay happens when an unstable nucleus (an isotope with the "wrong" ratio of protons to neutrons) emits particles or energy to become more stable. Alpha decay reduces both the atomic number and mass number; beta decay changes a neutron into a proton (or vice versa) without changing the mass number at all.
