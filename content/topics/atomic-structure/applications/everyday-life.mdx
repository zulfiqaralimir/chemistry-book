## Everyday Life — Smoke Detectors and Salt

Many household smoke detectors contain a tiny amount of **americium-241**, an isotope that emits radiation used to detect smoke particles interrupting a current. The device works because of exactly the nuclear behaviour introduced in this chapter — an unstable nucleus giving off radiation as it moves toward a more stable configuration.

Ordinary table salt (sodium chloride) is a everyday example of ion formation from Chapter 5: sodium atoms lose an electron to become Na$^+$ and chlorine atoms gain one to become Cl$^-$, and it's the strong attraction between these oppositely charged ions that holds the crystal together — and gives salt its high melting point compared to substances made of neutral molecules.
