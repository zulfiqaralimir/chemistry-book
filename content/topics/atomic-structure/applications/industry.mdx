## Industry — Nuclear Power and Isotope Enrichment

Nuclear power stations rely on **uranium-235**, an isotope that undergoes fission readily, while the far more abundant uranium-238 does not. Because the two isotopes are chemically identical, they can't be separated by any chemical reaction — instead, industrial **enrichment** processes (such as gas centrifuges) rely purely on the small mass difference between the isotopes described in Chapter 3.

Understanding electron configuration (Chapter 4) also underpins materials engineering: an element's reactivity, conductivity, and bonding behaviour — all consequences of its outer-shell electron arrangement — determine what it's industrially useful for, from the silicon used in semiconductors to the reactive metals used in batteries.
