## Answers

**Foundation**

1. Protons, neutrons, electrons.
2. Proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1.
3. In the nucleus, at the centre of the atom.
4. Because the number of protons (positive) equals the number of electrons (negative), so the charges cancel.
5. 12 electrons.

**Extended**

1. Protons = 13, neutrons = $27 - 13 = 14$, electrons = 13.
2. Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) with different numbers of neutrons, and therefore different mass numbers.
3. Similarity: both have 6 protons (same chemical properties). Difference: carbon-14 has 2 more neutrons than carbon-12 (8 vs. 6), and is radioactive while carbon-12 is stable.
4. a. Magnesium: 2, 8, 2. b. Fluorine: 2, 7.
5. Fluorine has 7 electrons in its outer shell — one short of a full shell of 8 — so it reacts readily by gaining one electron to complete its outer shell.

**Advanced**

1. $A_r = \dfrac{(63 \times 69.2) + (65 \times 30.8)}{100} = \dfrac{4359.6 + 2002}{100} = 63.6$
2. Magnesium loses its 2 outer electrons to form Mg$^{2+}$, with electron configuration 2, 8 (a full outer shell).
3. Ion has $16 + 2 = 18$ electrons (the extra electrons are the −2 charge). Electron configuration: 2, 8, 8.
4. Isotopes share the same number of protons and electrons, so their electron configurations — and therefore chemical bonding behaviour — are identical. But nuclear stability depends on the neutron-to-proton ratio in the nucleus, which differs between isotopes, so some ratios are stable while others are not.
